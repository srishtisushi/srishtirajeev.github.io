My name is Srishti, but I go by Sri. I'm a senior and a Stamps Scholar at the University of Arizona studying bioinformatics and biology, with minors in consciousness studies, ethics, and biochemistry. In short, I love science and technology, but I also love people.

{{< carousel "questions" >}}

I’m currently learning about interpretability, which I think has applications across several domains I’m interested in: philosophy of mind, AI safety, medical diagnosis, and bioinformatics. I’ve explored a lot over the past four years, but most of my work falls into two broad categories:

## computational neuroscience and biology

{{< split "neuro" >}}

At UofA, I've helped found the Consciousness Studies Club, conducted research on computational cognition with the [Bever Lab](https://dingo.sbs.arizona.edu/~tgb/index.html) (presented at [The Science of Consciousness Conference](https://consciousness.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2025-06/PROGRAM-June-30.pdf)), interviewed really cool folks (Neuralink patients, biocomputing researchers, etc.), and received a $10k grant to form a [brain-computer interface (BCI) lab](https://github.com/UA-BCILab) on campus (which has now grown to 12 undergraduates)! At the BCI Lab, I'm exploring concept activation vectors (CAVs) to understand which features drive the predictions of our motor activity decoding CNN and how they relate to underlying cognitive processes.

I also lead NASA's Open Science Data Repository's [Hydrocephalus Analysis Working Group](https://github.com/OpenScienceDataRepo/Brain_AWG/tree/main/Hydrocephalus), where we investigate how the molecular mechanisms driving hydrocephalus in space might inform our understanding of hydrocephalus on Earth. This interest grew out of my continued research on neurological disease at The Translational Genomics Research Institute (TGen) and [hydrocephalus research](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000211379) at Barrow Neurological Institute.

## systems-building & governance

{{< split "systems" >}}

Big fan of root cause analysis, and building systems where they don't exist but should. If you've seen people suffer because of something, and the issue is something you can prevent, why not intervene? 

At the International Rescue Committee – Tucson, I've worked on [improving healthcare policy for refugee children](https://southernazahec.org/2026-summit/elrio-posterfair/digital-poster-hall/) and [building dashboards](https://refugeesociety.org/wp-content/uploads/irmhc-2026-agenda-program-schedule.pdf) to map out the health needs and distribution of TB across our clients, which prompted a similar effort at IRC-Phoenix.

I also contribute to the UN's [Space and Global Health Network](https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/psa/globalhealth/index.html) and have attended multiple delegations, which has included [writing material](https://www.unoosa.org/res/oosadoc/data/documents/2026/aac_105c_12026crp/aac_105c_12026crp_24_0_html/AC105_C1_2026_CRP24E.pdf) on aerospace medicine, using satellite data for tracking vector-borne diseases, and the biosecurity concerns that arise from space biology research.

## previously

Many of these interests stem from surviving meningitis in an underfunded hospital in India when I was nine (fun fact: I woke up mid-anesthesia during a lumbar puncture - I’m okay now, don’t worry). Illness, even as a child, kind of forces you to think about life, suffering, and the nature of existence.

Back in high school, I worked on multi-omics and bioinformatics research on [cancer](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2023.41.16_suppl.1037) as both a [Helios](https://www.tgen.org/intern-profiles/2023/srishti-rajeev/) and [Ivy Neurological Sciences Scholar](https://ivyfoundation.org/opportunities/ivy-neurological-science-internship-program/) at the Translational Genomics Research Institute (TGen), was a two-time [Genes in Space semifinalist](https://www.genesinspace.org/news/blog/announcing-the-2021-finalists-and-honorable-mentions/), and conducted research on developing [bioinspired aerogels](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/aesr.202500293) at ASU. Truly grateful for the people I've met and the opportunities I've had.
